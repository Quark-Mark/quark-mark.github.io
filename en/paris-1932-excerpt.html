Paris 1932 — A Diary

Diary

Paris, 1 December 1932

It is truly difficult to paint Paris in acrylics — to compress the spirit of Paris into a single picture. Spirit is elusive even in a human being, let alone in Paris. Restless and broadly viscous. Different from the materiality of the soul, more versatile and more connected.

I began carefully on many canvases, only to turn each one, dissatisfied with what I saw, into a new picture: the portrait of a young lady, or one of those sugary motifs with a river and trees, an exterior of nature. Heavy kitsch. But people mostly like that kind of kitsch.

Then I admitted to myself: Paris cannot fit into a single picture. A frame is the physical boundary of every painting, but the picture of Paris has no frame.

Perhaps photographs could capture the outlines of the city’s spirit, if only for an instant. That modern art is still a mystery to me. But the whole process stirred something stranger in me: I began collecting images of Paris within myself, remembering it through my own eyes.

I walk along Rue Drouot, listening to the steps of art dealers whose shoes seem to press history into the stone. I turn into Rue Saint-Augustin, where silence tastes of ink and cold dust from archive folders. I pass through the Passage des Panoramas, an old corridor that swallows light as though afraid of being too modern for its century. I emerge on Boulevard Haussmann and the window of a perfume shop blinds me, the world reflected in it as though Paris were trying to understand itself through its own brilliance.

And then I understand: every one of these streets is my Paris. And something deeper: I am Paris while Quark Mark walks through its streets.

Paris is hundreds of pictures attaching themselves to me without asking.

To gather such images of myself, I must divide my own attention. A special kind of mental effort.

Quark Mark
