The Glass Snail · literary excerpt

The Glass Snail

The Glass Snail grew out of a literary game: before reading Milorad Pavić’s original stories, the author wrote his own versions guided only by their titles and by intuition. The result is a time-shifted dialogue between two writers, where posthumous communication becomes a tree growing between two inner worlds — the one that has departed and the one that remains to write.

The glass snail slept inside a transparent dream.

In the dream it heard the words of Pavić’s ghost, but wove them into the dream, tucking their meanings into the folds of two clocks uttering tick-tock.

The glass snail did not care what content was taken out of it or placed inside it, as long as it fulfilled its essence as a vessel. It existed only to contain — or not to contain — and nothing else. Centuries had taught it to have as few longing desires as possible, because desire leads to repetition. Repetition is slavery. Slavery is illusion. Illusion is pain.

The glass snail remembered. It remembered the time before it became a snail.

Exactly three thousand seven hundred years earlier, in the age of the first great prophet of Ahura Mazda, the glass snail had been a man. He lived beside a priest of strange customs...

Names meant repetition. It remembered having a vision of the future forms it would inhabit, and knew that once it became a glass snail it would be cleansed of all desire. To have no desire means to have neither pain nor joy. It had only one desire left — to have no more desires.

Repetition is slavery.
